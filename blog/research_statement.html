Research Statement

Thank you for coming to this page. I guess you are curious about what I mean by both perspectives. Here you go:
(i) As a grad student, I think about how hard it is to give every demographic group in a dataset a comparable level of privacy without losing a drastic amount of accuracy for any of them. It’s a well-documented problem, and it’s still not solved. Privacy-enhancing technologies (PETs) often cost some groups more accuracy than others, and some also hurt a model’s overall accuracy. Applying a PET can also make a model more open to adversarial manipulation. On top of that, we have to consider data dependency before deploying certain PETs, because some data, like time-series, makes privacy guarantees much harder to provide in the first place.

(ii) As an everyday user, I run into a different kind of uphill battle. I don’t want apps or websites to know more than they need to deliver the service I’m using, but the systems make that hard. They keep pushing me to turn on my location or accept all the cookies, even when all I want is to read an academic paper. Protecting my privacy takes way more effort than giving it up.

For me, these two lenses tell the same story: privacy is possible in theory, but it’s costly in practice, both for the people building these systems and the people using them. So my focus is on ensuring "Practical Privacy"
